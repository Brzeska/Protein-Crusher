RELEVANT PAPERS NOT YET INCORPORATED
Compiled and checked: 2026-11-03

Scope: additional reading relevant to the current tau/MAP2 stack analysis.
These studies have not supplied adopted parameters or analysis steps. Notes
describe why they may be useful and where their conclusions do not transfer.

1. Duan, P., Dregni, A. J., El Mammeri, N., & Hong, M. (2023).
   Structure of the nonhelical filament of the Alzheimer's disease tau core.
   Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 120(44), e2310067120.
   https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2310067120
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10622913/

   Relevance: Solid-state NMR supports a positively charged H329 and a
   protonated, neutral E338 in a nonhelical tau assembly grown under acidic
   conditions. Shows how local packing and solution conditions can change
   protonation and fibril structure. It does not establish the histidine
   states of 5O3L at neuronal pH.
   Citation correction: This is the paper previously called “Dregni et al.”
   in our conversation. Its first author is Duan; Dregni is second author.

2. Davidson, D. S., Brown, A. M., & Lemkul, J. A. (2018).
   Insights into Stabilizing Forces in Amyloid Fibrils of Differing Sizes
   from Polarizable Molecular Dynamics Simulations.
   Journal of Molecular Biology, 430(20), 3819–3834.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2018.05.020
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29782833/

   Relevance: Simulates amyloid fibrils, including AD tau, to examine how
   electrostatics, water, and interfaces contribute to structural stability.
   A useful precedent for investigating stack size and environmental effects.
   It uses a polarizable model, which we have not selected. The available
   methods excerpt identifies 5O3L and 5O3T; its complete histidine assignment
   was not verified in our literature check and should not be inferred.

3. Duan, P., El Mammeri, N., & Hong, M. (2024).
   Milligram-scale assembly and NMR fingerprint of tau fibrils adopting the
   Alzheimer's disease fold.
   Journal of Biological Chemistry, 300(6), 107326.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2024.107326
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11145547/

   Relevance: Provides experimental methods and NMR fingerprints for
   recombinant tau adopting the AD-like core fold. Useful for distinguishing
   a shared core shape from different filament packing and for seeing how
   preparation conditions affect the resulting structure. We have not
   extracted a complete histidine protonation map from this paper.

4. Charafeddine, R. A., Cortopassi, W. A., Lak, P., Tan, R., McKenney, R. J.,
   Jacobson, M. P., Barber, D. L., & Wittmann, T. (2019).
   Tau repeat regions contain conserved histidine residues that modulate
   microtubule-binding in response to changes in pH.
   Journal of Biological Chemistry, 294(22), 8779–8790.
   https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.RA118.007004
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6552421/

   Relevance: Demonstrates that histidines can make tau interactions
   sensitive to pH. Useful biological background for taking protonation
   seriously. The system is tau bound to microtubules, not an AD fibril;
   its protonation behavior should not be transferred directly to our stack.

5. Lövestam, S., Koh, F. A., van Knippenberg, B., Kotecha, A., Murzin, A. G.,
   Goedert, M., & Scheres, S. H. W. (2022).
   Assembly of recombinant tau into filaments identical to those of
   Alzheimer's disease and chronic traumatic encephalopathy.
   eLife, 11, e76494.
   https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.76494
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8983045/

   Relevance: Shows that shortened recombinant tau can form disease-relevant
   folds under particular laboratory conditions. Useful context for our
   core-only working hypothesis and for the role of salts and conditions in
   selecting folds. It does not by itself validate our exact capped fragment,
   finite stack, or chosen simulation environment.

6. Olsson, M. H. M., Søndergaard, C. R., Rostkowski, M., & Jensen, J. H. (2011).
   PROPKA3: Consistent Treatment of Internal and Surface Residues in Empirical
   pKa Predictions.
   Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation, 7(2), 525–537.
   https://doi.org/10.1021/ct100578z

   Relevance: Describes a tool that estimates how the local protein structure
   shifts residue pKa values. Could help narrow plausible protonation choices
   before a sensitivity study. Estimates are model-dependent; they do not
   experimentally determine the states or by themselves resolve every
   neutral-histidine tautomer choice. PROPKA has not been run for this project.

7. Søndergaard, C. R., Olsson, M. H. M., Rostkowski, M., & Jensen, J. H. (2011).
   Improved Treatment of Ligands and Coupling Effects in Empirical Calculation
   and Rationalization of pKa Values.
   Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation, 7(7), 2284–2295.
   https://doi.org/10.1021/ct200133y

   Relevance: Companion PROPKA methods paper covering refinements including
   coupling between ionizable groups. Relevant if nearby histidines or other
   charged residues influence one another. The PROPKA maintainers request
   citation of both this paper and Olsson et al. if their tool is used.
   Citation guidance: https://github.com/jensengroup/propka

MOVE TO THE CURRENT-PROJECT LIST WHEN APPROPRIATE
